Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball: Which Strategy Saves More?

When you are determined to eliminate credit card debt and personal loans, choosing the right repayment framework can save you thousands of dollars and months of payments.

1. The Core Difference at a Glance

Both strategies require you to pay the minimum monthly payment on every single debt to protect your credit score. The difference is solely how you deploy your extra cash (e.g., an extra $100, $250, or $500 per month).

⚡ Debt Avalanche

Target the debt with the highest APR first. Minimizes total interest paid to lenders.

⛄ Debt Snowball

Target the debt with the smallest balance first. Delivers rapid psychological wins.

2. A Real Mathematical Case Study

Suppose an American household has three debts totaling $18,000:

  • Credit Card A: $3,000 @ 26.9% APR (Min Pay: $90/mo)
  • Credit Card B: $5,000 @ 22.4% APR (Min Pay: $130/mo)
  • Personal Loan: $10,000 @ 9.5% APR (Min Pay: $250/mo)

If they add an extra $200/month toward payoff:

Minimum Payments Only: Debt-free in 6.5 Years | $9,420 Interest
Debt Snowball Method: Debt-free in 2.8 Years | $3,640 Interest
Debt Avalanche Method: Debt-free in 2.6 Years | $3,180 Interest (Saves +$460)

3. Which One Should You Choose?

Choose the Debt Avalanche if you are motivated by pure numbers, spreadsheets, and optimizing every cent of interest.

Choose the Debt Snowball if you have felt overwhelmed in the past or have multiple small lingering accounts ($500 medical bill, $800 store card) where eliminating an entire bill from your mailbox provides mental breathing room.
